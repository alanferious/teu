# TEU: IB Economics HL revision

TEU (twenty-foot equivalent unit) is a revision app for IB Economics HL. The Unit 4 examples are stacked as shipping containers on a bay plan, Unit 3 is a stock market app, Unit 2 is an airport terminal printed like a travel poster, and Unit 4's notes are a nautical chart of the world economy.

Use it online at **https://alanferious.github.io/teu/**, or download this folder and open `index.html` in Chrome, Edge or Firefox. Nothing to set up. Online, it keeps working without a connection after your first visit.

- **Course notes**: all 31 subtopics in Units 1–4, with 336 definitions, 76 diagrams with drawing steps, formulas, memory aids, evaluation points and exam traps. HL-only content is marked.
  Modes: Notes (with search), Flashcards, Diagram drill and Term quiz.
- **Unit 2 airport**: Unit 2 is a terminal. The departures board is the Unit 2 home: every subtopic is a flight (TU 201–212) from a gate (A1–A6 for markets, B7–B12 for government and market failure), with a status that comes from your flashcards: *Boarding* when cards are due, *Delayed* when they're overdue, *Check-in open* when you haven't started, *Departed* when every term is mastered. Each gate page has the whole subtopic: the passenger list (definitions, weakest first), key ideas, exam traps as gate announcements, diagrams, formulas, memory aids and the gate's real-world examples. Its terms sit in the lounge until you master them; then they board the plane and light a cabin window. When the last one boards, the plane pushes back and taxis away, once, and the gate is stamped in your Unit 2 passport. Flashcards become boarding passes, and every grade earns miles towards Silver and Gold.
  - **Unit 2 examples (duty free)**: 28 real-world examples for micro, two or three per gate, from Nigeria's petrol subsidy and Barcelona's rent cap to Japan's rice price spike and the EU's €890m fine on Google. None are US-centred, and they come with the same story, three numbers and "but". They are easy to reach: the departures board lists every one by gate (one tap opens it at its gate), each gate page shows its examples near the top, and the **Examples** tab (shown in Unit 2 and Unit 3) opens on Browse, every example grouped by gate, before the drills: Recall with a tick list, Numbers and Pick the example (<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd>/<kbd>R</kbd>/<kbd>N</kbd>/<kbd>P</kbd>). In Unit 2 notes, <kbd>D</kbd> opens the gate's flashcards and <kbd>E</kbd> its examples.
- **Unit 4 chart**: Unit 4's notes are an old sea chart. The Unit 4 home is the Chart of the World Economy: one port of call per subtopic, each a real harbour tied to that subtopic's flagship example (Valparaíso for Chile's cherries, Yokohama for the yen, Chattogram for Bangladesh's garments). A port's marker comes from your flashcards: anchored once every term is mastered, a magenta light when cards are due, a danger mark when overdue. A ship sits at the next port to study, the most overdue one first. Each subtopic page is a chart plate with its real-world examples pinned on their countries (click a pin to open the example), then the full notes. Flashcards become entries in the log of the TEU Meridian, and every grade logs nautical miles. On phones the subtopic table leads and the chart is one tap away. In Unit 4 notes, <kbd>D</kbd> opens the flashcards.
- **Unit 3 stocks**: Unit 3 looks like a stocks app. Each subtopic is a stock (MEAS, ADAS, OBJ, INEQ, MONP, FISC, SUPP) and so is the Unit 3 index (TEU3). Each one has a chart, stats, its terms, diagrams, formulas and "top stories" (key ideas, exam traps, evaluation points). Every term's price is how well you recall it. It lists at 10.00 the first time you grade it, rises each time you recall it, and drifts down once it is overdue.
  - **The game**: in Flashcards, call each Unit 3 card before you flip it: *Buy* if you know it, *Sell* if you're not sure. Your call stays hidden until you've graded, so grade against the wording, not your guess. You get a trade confirmation after every grade (<kbd>U</kbd> undoes it) and a "Closing bell" summary at the end of a session, with the terms you missed ready to drill again. The TEU3 page shows your exam target (70.00 by Paper 2), overdue terms that are falling, today's top gainers, your trading-day run, how well calibrated your calls are, and 12 records to earn.
  - **Unit 3 examples (RWE3)**: 26 real-world examples for macro, about four per subtopic, from Ireland's leprechaun GDP to Turkey's 50% interest rate and India's phone-making subsidies. None are US-centred. They use the same memory card as the Unit 4 bank: a story, three numbers, what to use it for, and one "but". Each example is a stock too: it lists at 10.00 and rises as you recall it. The **Examples** tab (shown in Unit 3) has three drills: Recall, Numbers and Pick the example (<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd>/<kbd>N</kbd>/<kbd>P</kbd>). In Recall you make a Buy/Sell call, flip, tick what you got (the story, each number, the "but", keys <kbd>Q</kbd>–<kbd>T</kbd>) and TEU suggests a grade you accept with <kbd>Enter</kbd>. New examples come in batches of six. Drill fronts show a short cue, never the figures. Numbers and Pick the example are practice and don't move prices. Every subtopic page lists its own examples, plus ones from other subtopics that also fit.
- **Evidence bank**: 42 real-world examples for Unit 4, rated on relevance, evaluation, data and recency. Each one is boiled down to a story, three key numbers and one "but".
  Modes: Library (the bay plan), Drill, Number recall, Pick the example, and Write & check (an offline checker for Paper 1 (b)-style paragraphs).

In the Library the examples are containers on a ship at the quay. Click one and the crane lifts it out for you. A weathered container is an example you haven't drilled yet. A freshly painted one is one you're learning, and a sealed one is mastered. A yellow corner means it is due for review.

Keys: <kbd>Space</kbd> flips a card, <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd> grade it or pick an answer, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> moves on. On Unit 3 cards, <kbd>B</kbd> and <kbd>S</kbd> buy or sell before flipping. In Unit 3 notes, <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> move through the stocks, <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>5</kbd> change the chart range, <kbd>D</kbd> drills and <kbd>/</kbd> searches.

Progress is saved in your browser (localStorage). To move it to another computer, open **Your progress** at the bottom of the page and use **Copy progress**, then **Add to my progress** on the other one.

## Notes

The course notes follow the IB Economics guide (first assessment 2022), but they are not official IB material. Check them against your class notes. Facts in the evidence bank were checked against the linked sources as of 26 October 2026, the Unit 3 examples as of 28 October 2026, and the Unit 2 examples as of 29 October 2026. The ratings are our own, not examiner scores. Not affiliated with the International Baccalaureate.
